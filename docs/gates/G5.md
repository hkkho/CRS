# G5 - Phase 5 refuelling and depletion review

## Result

`PASS` for the approved `ReducedModel` / engine-neutral Core scope after the
same-context high-effort correction review. Direct production/external CANDU
authority remains deferred and is not included in this disposition.

The initial G5 evidence covered the declarative schemes, atomic directional
movement, fresh insertion and discharge, explicit-time burnup/energy
accounting, coefficient lookup and recompute contracts, identity/location
invariants, deterministic multistep histories, complete I/Xe/lifecycle/
power-history state, and the P5-T10 structural sequence consumer bound to the
G4-R6-approved ReducedModel artifact. The reviewer found that the accepted
complete-state burnup path could preserve stale current power and coefficient
bindings, and that P5-T10 was being overstated as independent numerical burnup
validation. P5-T11 addressed those findings, but the correction review found
two further High bypasses: missing source inventory/burnup-energy digest
authentication and a successful no-table complete-refuelling overload. P5-T12
addressed those findings. The next same-context review found one Medium
bypass: the burnup boundary authenticated only the latest power-history record
instead of the complete append-only history. P5-T13 addressed that finding.
The fourth review then found a High bypass: the accepted inventory digest was
caller-controlled because it was not stored in lifecycle-authenticated state.
P5-T14 implemented that binding. The fifth review found a High persistence
bypass: the lifecycle-authenticated snapshot digests were included in the
canonical state hash but were omitted from archive write/read/restore, so a
save/load could silently lose the binding. P5-T15 implemented the explicit
schema-v2 persistence correction, but the same review also found that restore
did not reconcile the restored complete inventory and burnup/energy state
against the authenticated digests, and that the post-load regression reused
the pre-save snapshot object. P5-T16 implements those restore-authentication
and restart-reconstruction corrections; the final same-context review returned
`PASS` with no remaining actionable findings.

This is not a direct CANDU, DONJON5, DRAGON5, full-core, release, safety, or
mobile approval. Those authorities and evidence remain outside the admitted
scope and are preserved as deferred work.

## Findings

Initial same-context review findings:

- High: the accepted complete-state burnup path could preserve stale
  `PowerWatts`, `PowerSnapshotId`, and `CoefficientBinding` through
  `BundleState.WithEnergy`, and it did not provide the required atomic
  complete snapshot/lifecycle burnup commit with every next-state lookup,
  lifecycle time/core advancement, invalidation, and rollback evidence.
- Medium: P5-T10 constructed cumulative energy directly from the approved
  ReducedModel burnup values, so it proves structural mapping and identity/
  accounting preservation rather than independent power-to-burnup integration
  or accumulated numerical drift.

Corrective status: `P5-T11` implements the complete-state transaction,
invalidates legacy energy bindings, adds strict post-shift refuelling lookups,
and adds composed rollback/stale-binding/deterministic-history tests. P5-T10's
  report and scope entry now explicitly use the structural-mapping disposition.
  Resolved; final same-context G5 review PASS.
The second review then found: (a) no authenticated canonical source inventory
and burnup/energy digests on the accepted snapshot/complete burnup boundary,
and (b) the public no-table complete-refuelling overload could still commit
without post-shift bindings. `P5-T12` adds both digest checks and makes that
path fail closed. Resolved; final same-context G5 review PASS.
The third review found one Medium bypass: `CompleteStateDigestV1.ComputeInventory`
did not authenticate the full append-only history at the burnup boundary, so
an earlier record could be removed while the latest record remained. `P5-T13`
adds separate full pre-accept and post-accept inventory digests, verifies the
post-accept digest before burnup construction, and adds the required regression.
Resolved; final same-context G5 review PASS.
The fourth review found one High bypass: the accepted inventory digest was
caller-controlled because it was not stored in lifecycle-authenticated state.
`P5-T14` stores the three snapshot state digests on the accepted lifecycle,
includes them in the canonical lifecycle projection, and adds a forged
inventory-plus-snapshot regression. Resolved; final same-context G5 review
PASS.
The fifth review found one High persistence bypass: the three lifecycle digest
fields were included in canonical lifecycle hashing but were omitted from
`StateArchiveCodecV1.WriteLifecycle`/`ReadLifecycle` and restore, so a complete
accepted snapshot lost its authenticated tuple across save/load. `P5-T15`
advances the lifecycle/snapshot/archive schema boundary to v2, persists all
three fields plus the full inventory projection, and adds a round-trip test
that performs a subsequent burnup. The same review also found: (a) High,
restore accepted a tampered complete archive without reconciling the restored
inventory and burnup/energy state to the lifecycle-authenticated digests; and
(b) Medium, the post-load test reused the pre-save snapshot object rather than
reconstructing it from restored state. `P5-T16` adds restore-time digest and
per-bundle coherence validation, tampered-archive rejection, and
restart-self-contained snapshot reconstruction. Resolved; final same-context G5
review PASS.
